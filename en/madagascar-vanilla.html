What is Madagascar Bourbon vanilla?

Vanilla marketed as Bourbon vanilla from Madagascar is mainly produced from Vanilla planifolia. For professional buyers, origin alone is not enough: curing, sorting, maturity, moisture, bean length and lot condition determine whether the product is suitable for the intended use.

Grade A / Gourmet

Whole beans for premium food, pastry, gastronomy, specialist retail and applications where bean presentation is important.

Grade B / Extraction

Vanilla for extractors, flavour manufacturers and industrial applications, where technical and analytical requirements may matter more than appearance.

Quality criteria professional buyers should check

  • Grade and intended use: gourmet, extraction or another technical requirement;
  • Length: usually stated as a bean-length range;
  • Moisture: confirmed according to the lot and intended use;
  • Appearance: flexibility, integrity, colour and consistency;
  • Analysis: lot specification or COA where available or required;
  • Packing: professional export packing, vacuum packing or custom format.

For a fuller qualification method, see our vanilla quality & documents guide.

Why does Madagascar vanilla pricing vary?

Madagascar vanilla pricing depends on lot quality, volume, sorting and packing requirements, certifications where applicable, documentation, destination and shipping terms. A price is only truly comparable when the underlying specification is equivalent.

Exporting vanilla from Madagascar may involve commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, phytosanitary documentation, lot specifications or other documents requested by the importer, depending on the shipment and destination.

What should wholesalers, importers and distributors include in an enquiry?

Provide your company name, required quantity, grade, preferred bean length, target moisture if applicable, packing, certifications if any, destination country or port and expected purchasing frequency. Our importer checklist helps structure this request.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Grade A and Grade B?

Grade A is generally intended for gourmet and premium applications; Grade B is more commonly used for extraction and industrial processing. Exact specifications are confirmed lot by lot.

Is there one fixed Madagascar vanilla price?

No. Price varies with lot quality, specification, volume, packing, destination and commercial terms.

Can buyers request quality documents?

Yes. Depending on availability and requirements, lot specifications, COA or other documents can be supplied or arranged case by case.
