How to verify a Madagascar vanilla supplier

Before a first order, professional buyers should verify the seller’s identity, the consistency of the offered lot, applicable documents, payment terms and logistics. This checklist helps reduce risk in international purchasing.

1. Verify the seller’s legal identity

Request the exact legal name, address, registration references, professional contact details and contact person. Check that commercial documents use the same identity.

2. Request an offer linked to an actual lot

A useful offer should state at least grade, quantity, relevant length range, requested moisture, packing, destination and commercial terms.

3. Verify documents that actually apply

Do not assume a certificate exists because it is mentioned in general marketing. Ask which documents apply to the specific lot: specification, COA when available or required, relevant lot certificate, export documents and company records.

4. Request a sample when appropriate

For a new relationship, a sample or trial order can help verify appearance, aroma, length, flexibility, packing and consistency with the agreed specification.

5. Clarify payment and beneficiary

The payment beneficiary should be consistent with the company or with a clearly explained commercial structure. Be cautious with unexpected bank-detail changes or unusual urgency.

6. Clarify Incoterm and transport

Ask what the price includes: origin, main carriage, possible insurance, delivery to port or another point. Responsibilities should be explicit.

7. Cross-check external presence

Search the company in registries, professional directories, industry lists and search engines. Challenge Food SARL, for example, appears in the “New list 2026” published by MadagascarVanilla.com. Third-party presence is an additional signal, not a guarantee on its own.

8. Keep a written audit trail

Archive quotations, specifications, attachments, lot confirmations and payment terms. Clear documentation protects both parties.

This page provides a general due diligence method and does not replace legal, regulatory or banking checks appropriate to your company and destination market.
