What makes a vanilla bean “best”?

For professional buyers, quality is not one single number. The most suitable bean combines origin, botanical variety, curing quality, moisture, length, flexibility, aroma, visual condition and intended use. Madagascar Bourbon vanilla, generally Vanilla planifolia, is widely used across gourmet and extraction applications.

Grade A / Gourmet

Usually chosen where appearance, suppleness and whole-bean presentation matter: pastry, gastronomy, premium retail and visible culinary use.

Grade B / Extraction

Usually chosen for extract, flavour manufacturing and industrial processing where the bean is transformed rather than sold for visual presentation.

Best vanilla beans for baking and pastry

For pastry, restaurants and premium retail, buyers often prefer flexible whole beans with a clean appearance and a strong aromatic profile. Grade A or Gourmet beans are commonly requested for these applications. The exact length and moisture should still be confirmed lot by lot.

Best vanilla beans for extract

For extraction, appearance is less important than suitability for processing, cost efficiency and the agreed analytical specification. Grade B or extraction-grade vanilla is therefore commonly requested by extractors and flavour manufacturers.

How professional buyers should compare suppliers

  • confirm the exact origin and botanical variety;
  • request lot-specific grade, length and moisture information;
  • ask for COA or analytical documentation when needed;
  • confirm packing, vacuum packaging and export documentation;
  • verify certification claims against the exact lot rather than assuming all products are certified;
  • compare the total landed terms, not only the quoted price per kilogram.
“Best vanilla beans” should describe suitability for a use case, not an unsupported claim that one supplier or lot is universally superior.
