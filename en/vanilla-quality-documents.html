Lot characteristics to confirm

Grade

Grade A/Gourmet, Grade B/Extraction or another category depending on intended use and actual availability.

Length

A defined length range helps buyers compare equivalent offers and plan suitable packing.

Moisture

Moisture affects flexibility, weight, storage and suitability for specific professional uses.

Appearance & preparation

Color, flexibility, pod integrity, sorting and preparation should match the agreed specification.

COA and analysis

A Certificate of Analysis (COA) may be requested when available or required. Depending on the buyer’s needs, parameters may include moisture, vanillin or other analytical criteria. Specific analysis requirements should be stated before quotation so feasibility and cost can be confirmed.

Company and lot documentation

For supplier qualification, buyers may request corporate registration information, lot specifications, available quality documents and certificates that actually apply to the product or lot offered.

Challenge Food does not present a certification or analysis as confirmed unless it applies to the relevant lot. Special requirements are checked before commitment.

Shipment documents

Depending on destination and transaction, documentation may include commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, applicable phytosanitary documents and other items required by the importer. Exact obligations vary by market.

Professional packing

Packing should be defined according to quantity, grade, transport duration and buyer requirements. Vacuum packing and professional export packing can be discussed during quotation.

Useful questions before approving a supplier

  • Do the specifications match the actual lot offered?
  • Can the seller clearly identify its company and country of origin?
  • Are requested documents available for this specific lot?
  • Are packing and Incoterm clearly defined?
  • Have destination-market requirements been checked?
