Why Madagascar vanilla prices vary

A vanilla price is meaningful only when it is tied to a defined specification. Comparing a price per kilogram without checking grade, moisture, length, packing, documents and shipping terms may mean comparing very different offers.

Lot quality

Grade, appearance, flexibility, bean length, moisture and requested analytical characteristics affect selection and commercial value.

Volume

Samples, trial orders and recurring supply programmes involve different preparation, packing and logistics requirements.

Packing

Vacuum packing, unit size, export cartons, labels and custom requirements should be included in the quotation basis.

Destination

Destination country, transport method, Incoterm and documentation influence the total commercial offer.

Grade A and Grade B are priced for different uses

Grade A vanilla used for gourmet or premium presentation is selected differently from Grade B extraction vanilla. Buyers should therefore request a price for a defined use and specification rather than a generic vanilla price.

Certifications, analysis and documents

If your company requires a certification, COA, supplier qualification documents, special analysis or destination-specific paperwork, include those requirements in the enquiry. Availability is confirmed for the relevant lot and product.

Challenge Food does not publish one permanent fixed vanilla price. Quotations are based on the actual lot, quantity, specification and destination so the commercial figure corresponds to the buyer's real requirement.

How to request a comparable quotation

  • Grade A, Grade B or other required specification;
  • quantity and expected purchasing frequency;
  • preferred bean length or range when relevant;
  • target moisture if required;
  • packing format;
  • certification and analytical requirements;
  • destination country or port;
  • preferred Incoterm or request for a logistics proposal.

FOB, CIF and other commercial terms

The quoted amount also depends on how far the seller's responsibilities extend. An origin-based offer and an offer including international transport are not equivalent. Stating the preferred Incoterm helps clarify which costs and responsibilities are included.
